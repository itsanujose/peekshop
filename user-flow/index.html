Know Before You Go. Shop Smart. Save Time. Feel Fine.

Three connected journeys for the Berlin pilot: shoppers find products, recover from sold-out results and retailers keep shelf availability trustworthy.

01 / Core shopper journey

Core shopper journey

From search to shelf: account access, product search, availability, store selection, optional reservation and walking directions.

Returning shoppers go to Home. New shoppers can log in, register or use the demo. Location consent has a manual alternative. Check stock count and freshness, filter results, choose a store and optionally reserve for one hour. After the visit, confirm whether the product was on the shelf.

Backlog traceability: PS-E1–E6 · PS-E10

02 / Sold-out recovery

Sold-out recovery

Find a suitable alternative or avoid an unnecessary trip.

If a similar product is in stock, switch to that product and review its stores. Otherwise check other stores for the original item. If none are nearby, save the product for later.

Backlog traceability: PS-11 · PS-20

03 / Retailer stock update

Retailer stock update

Keep availability accurate through CSV, quick phone updates or pilot POS integration.

Approved stores publish stock with timestamps. Valid CSV rows are published; invalid rows show reasons. Stock older than 60 minutes during opening hours is flagged and the retailer reminded. A negative shelf confirmation also flags the record.

Backlog traceability: PS-21–PS-25 · PS-29

Shared handoffs

Shopper reserves → retailer sets item aside → pickup code validated → reservation marked collected. Cancellation or expiry releases the hold. A reminder is sent 15 minutes before expiry.

Retailer publishes inventory → shopper sees time-stamped availability → shopper confirms shelf accuracy → negative feedback flags the record and notifies the store.

MVP concept and prototype workflow. AI assistant, loyalty, promotions, payments and delivery are outside the MVP. Prototype sample stock is not live inventory.
