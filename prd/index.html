One Page for PeekShop

Overview

PeekShop is a mobile app that helps shoppers in Berlin know whether a product is on the shelf at a nearby local store before they leave home. A shopper searches for a product, sees which nearby stores have it right now (with a stock count and the time it was last updated), chooses the best store by distance, opening hours and crowd level, optionally reserves the item for one hour, follows walking directions and finds the product in the store by aisle and shelf.

On the other side, a simple retailer portal lets small independent stores keep stock accurate through a CSV upload or a quick update from their phone, with till (POS) integration piloted for stores that have one. The first release is a Berlin pilot in Neukölln, Kreuzberg, Wedding and Schöneberg, built around the specialty and community grocers (Indian, Turkish, Ukrainian, Italian, Spanish) whose stock is rarely visible online. PeekShop launches in English and German.

Product vision. To make everyday grocery shopping seamless, fast and rewarding by helping shoppers find what they need, know what’s available at nearby local stores before they go, navigate to the right store, find products easily in store, check crowd levels, avoid unnecessary trips and queues, and make smarter shopping decisions.

Problem

Shoppers find it hard to know whether a product is available in a nearby physical store before they visit. Product information, store inventory, promotions and loyalty services are spread across different channels, which fragments the experience and makes trips hard to plan. As a result, shoppers:

visit stores where the product is unavailable and make extra trips;

spend unnecessary time searching, calling ahead and asking friends;

miss relevant offers, then give up, substitute, or order online at higher cost;

drift to digital-native competitors that feel more convenient.

The problem is sharpest for people who rely on local specialty stores (Indian, Turkish, Ukrainian, Italian, Spanish and other community grocers in Berlin), whose stock is rarely visible online and changes with every shipment. In our interviews a missing brand of atta turned a 15-minute errand into most of a morning, and shoppers who phoned ahead still found items sold out. Uncertainty, not distance, is the real barrier: one shopper will travel 30 minutes if the product is guaranteed, but only 15 if unsure. These findings are directional and are re-tested in the assumptions listed under Other Considerations.

Objectives

One confident trip. Let a shopper in Berlin confirm in under two minutes (proposed target) that a product is on the shelf at a nearby store, choose where to go and get there, so an errand takes one trip, not three.

Trust in every number. Make stock data trustworthy: every count is time-stamped, stale data is flagged, and “Was it on the shelf?” answers keep accuracy honest (proposed target: 95%).

A supply network that works. Make it easy for small independent stores to share accurate stock (CSV, phone, till integration) and build a pilot network of 20–30 specialty stores across four Berlin districts.

Prove the value, then decide. Show that reliable local availability drives repeat use, learn what retailers would pay for, and end the pilot with a clear decision to scale, change course or stop.

Constraints

Supply first: The product is only as good as the stock data behind it. Shoppers should not be invited in until 20–30 pilot stores are live, so every search returns something useful, and stores need an update path that takes minutes, not hours.

Privacy and regulation: Location, account and reservation data are personal data. A GDPR (DSGVO) review is required before launch: explicit location consent with a manual alternative, data minimisation, data export and deletion, EU data hosting, and no personal data in analytics without consent.

MVP scope: Search, availability, store details, map, reserve and collect, crowd level and the retailer portal only. No delivery, payments, in-app purchase, AI assistant, loyalty integration or referral programme in this release.

Geography and language: Berlin only (Neukölln, Kreuzberg, Wedding, Schöneberg). English and German at launch, with text externalised for more languages.

Third-party dependencies: Map and directions, search, notifications, till (POS) systems and monitoring depend on providers chosen in Sprint 0. Each must degrade gracefully rather than block a shopper’s search.

Name and brand: PeekShop needs trademark clearance (DPMA, EUIPO, classes 9, 35 and 42) plus domain and app-store checks before public launch.

Evidence base: Several interview answers in the research workspace are persona role-play or dummy answers written to practise unbiased questioning. Treat the insights as directional until validated with real shoppers and stores.

Use Cases

Scenario 1 — Search to shelf, end to end

Raj needs a specific brand of whole wheat atta. Instead of driving to the Indian store on spec, he opens PeekShop, logs in and lands on Home for Neukölln, Berlin. He types “atta” and picks Whole wheat atta, 5 kg from the suggestions. The results list nearby stores with stock status, count, distance, hours and “Updated X min ago”. He turns on In stock and Open now, opens the closest store, checks the crowd level and taps Get directions. In the shop, the floor plan points him to Aisle 3, shelf B. Back in the app PeekShop asks “Was it on the shelf?” and he taps Yes.

Scenario 2 — Sold out, still one trip

Ananya, a student without a car, searches for her usual atta. The nearest store shows Sold out, but the card says “Similar here: Chakki atta is in stock”. She taps it, the results switch to the alternative and she checks its pack size before setting off. When no similar product is in stock nearby, she saves the product to check again later instead of making a wasted trip.

Scenario 3 — Reserve and send someone else

Lana, five months pregnant, cannot afford another failed trip. She finds Ukrainian black rye bread in stock, taps Reserve for 1 hour and receives a pickup code and a held-until time. Her partner collects it: he shows the code at the counter, staff enter it in the retailer portal and the reservation is marked collected. If plans change, Lana cancels with one tap and the item is released for others. If 60 minutes pass, the hold expires on its own, and she is reminded 15 minutes beforehand.

Scenario 4 — A late stop after a long shift

Emma finishes a hospital shift late and needs one item on the way home. She searches for it, switches on Open now and Quiet, and PeekShop shows only stores that are open and not busy. A store that has already closed is labelled Closed now and cannot be reserved. She picks the quietest store, walks there with the route on screen and is back home in minutes.

Scenario 5 — A store keeps its shelf honest

The owner of a small Turkish grocer completes his store profile with hours, address and photos and, once approved, appears in search. When a shipment arrives he uploads a CSV with product, quantity, aisle and shelf: valid rows are published and invalid rows are listed with reasons. During the day he marks a product as sold out from his phone and shoppers see it within a minute. If a record is not updated for 60 minutes during opening hours, shoppers see “May be outdated” and he receives a reminder. When a shopper answers No to “Was it on the shelf?”, the store is notified and the record is flagged.

How to try the prototype

The prototype works in any modern browser on a computer or phone. All stores, stock, times and pickup codes are sample data, and accounts exist only until the page is reloaded, so please don’t use a real password. Open interactive prototype

Open the prototype. A green splash screen with the PeekShop logo and “Know before you go” shows for about a second and a half, then the Welcome screen appears. Tap Fill in demo login, then Log in (demo@peekshop.app / peek1234). You arrive on Home, greeted as Raj.

Tap the search field and type a product such as “atta”, “bread”, “cheese” or “tomatoes”, then pick a suggestion or press Enter. You can also tap a category chip (Indian, Turkish, Ukrainian, Italian).

On the results screen check each store’s stock status, count and “Updated X min ago”. Use the In stock, Open now, Under 1 km and Quiet filters, and tap the green “Similar here” line on a sold-out store to switch product.

Open a store to see the crowd level, stock count and the aisle and shelf on the floor plan. Tap Reserve for 1 hour for a pickup code (tap again to cancel) and the heart to save the store.

Tap Get directions, then Start walking directions and Next step. On arrival PeekShop shows your pickup code or where to find the product.

Open Saved to see saved items. Tap the log-out icon (a door with an arrow) to return to Welcome; saved items and reservations are cleared.

Technical Considerations - (optional)

Out of scope for this draft by design: Engineering confirms the choices below in Sprint 0 through technical spikes on search, maps and POS integration. Current thinking, drawn from the backlog tasks and non-functional requirements:

Architecture (logical). Three parts: the shopper app (iOS 16+ and Android 10+), the retailer portal (web, on desktop and mobile browsers) and a backend that serves availability, search, reservations, crowd levels and analytics. Stock reaches the backend through three update paths: CSV upload, quick phone update and, for one pilot provider, till (POS) sync. Third-party services cover maps, notifications, analytics and crash monitoring. No providers are chosen yet.

Stock availability and freshness. Every stock record carries a source (CSV, phone or POS) and an updated-at timestamp. Portal changes reach shoppers within 1 minute and POS sync within 5. A staleness job flags records older than 60 minutes during opening hours as “May be outdated” and reminds the store. Stock queries are cached, and every portal update is written to an audit log.

Search. A product index with typo tolerance and German and community synonyms (for example atta and chakki atta). Suggestions appear within 300 ms and results within 2 seconds (p95).

Reservations. A reservation service with an expiry job: a 60-minute hold, a pickup code, and automatic release of stock on expiry or cancellation. Shoppers get a push notification 15 minutes before expiry and the store gets an email or SMS alert.

Maps and directions. A map SDK (Mapbox or Google Maps) chosen in Sprint 0 on cost, caching and usage limits. The app shows walking time and hands turn-by-turn navigation to the native maps app by deep link. The prototype uses an illustrated map.

Crowd level. The MVP uses a simple signal, either a level set by the store or a footfall counter, that expires after 15 minutes. The indicator is hidden rather than guessed when no recent data exists. Predicted crowd levels are out of scope.

Security, privacy and observability. Hashed passwords, HTTPS, login rate limiting and role-based access for retailer staff. Analytics events fire only after consent. Crash and performance monitoring with alerts on error spikes.

Prototype status. The prototype is front-end only: nothing is stored or sent anywhere, accounts live until reload and the data is sample data.

MVP hypothesis and exit criteria

Hypothesis: if shoppers can see trustworthy, time-stamped stock at nearby specialty stores and reserve an item, they will make fewer wasted trips and come back to PeekShop for repeat errands.

The MVP exits the pilot when all of the following hold:

At least 20 pilot stores are live, with stock updated in the last 60 minutes for 80% of opening hours.

Stock accuracy is 95% or higher from “Was it on the shelf?” answers (proposed target).

Activation and repeat-use baselines are established, with a decision to scale, change course or stop.

GTM Approach

Out of scope for this draft by design: the launch plan is developed with Marketing. The approach below is what the team has agreed so far.

Target segment. Busy working adults and time-conscious grocery shoppers in Berlin, aged roughly 18 to 60, especially expats and community shoppers who rely on local specialty stores. Secondary: owners of small independent grocery stores.

Rollout. A Berlin pilot in four districts (Neukölln, Kreuzberg, Wedding and Schöneberg), supply first: recruit 20–30 specialty stores across Indian, Turkish, Ukrainian, Italian and Spanish communities before opening to shoppers, so every search returns useful results. A concierge test with three stores comes first, to check that stores can keep counts accurate with CSV and phone updates.

Positioning.

For busy shoppers in Berlin who depend on local and specialty stores, who waste time on trips to stores that don’t have what they need, PeekShop is a mobile app that shows which nearby stores have a product in stock right now, how busy they are and where the item is on the shelf. Unlike retailer apps, leaflet apps or general product search, PeekShop combines accurate store-level stock with discovery across independent stores and help in getting the item in one trip.

Differentiation. Search, store finders and recommendations can be copied. PeekShop’s defensible combination is accurate store-level availability across independent stores, intelligent product discovery for community products, and help completing the trip (alternatives, reservation, aisle finder, crowd level). The retailer network and the stock-accuracy track record are the moat.

Q&A

Asked byQuestionAnswer
ProductWhy start with specialty and community stores instead of a retail chain?Interviews pointed to them as the sharpest and most frequent pain, and their stock is rarely visible online. Chains already cover their own stores, so focusing here avoids head-on competition and lets us move fast in one city.
DesignWhy does every stock count show “Updated X min ago”?Trust is the product. Showing “3 available” when the shelf has none destroys trust, so every count carries its age and data older than 60 minutes is flagged (PS-10, PS-25).
DesignWhy hide the crowd indicator instead of estimating it?If we don’t know, we say so rather than guess. With no recent signal the indicator is hidden (PS-26).
EngineeringWhy CSV upload and phone updates before POS integration?Small stores can start in minutes without a till integration. POS is a single-provider pilot (PS-24, Could) and is validated after a two-week concierge test with three stores.
ProductWhy a 60-minute hold?Interviewees will travel up to about 30 minutes when availability is guaranteed, so an hour covers a realistic trip. The hold expires automatically so stock is not blocked (PS-15).
Legal/PrivacyWhy is location optional, with a manual area picker?Location is personal data and must be requested with a clear reason (GDPR). Shoppers who decline can still choose an area, for example Neukölln (PS-3).
BusinessWhy no payments or delivery in the MVP?The MVP must prove that reliable availability drives repeat use. PeekShop competes on convenience, speed and availability, not on price, and delivery is a later partner-led option.
ResearchWhy treat the interview insights as directional?Several answers are persona role-play or dummy answers written to practise unbiased questioning. Real interviews with 8–10 shoppers and 5–6 retailers come before build.
BusinessWhy is PeekShop free for shoppers in the pilot?The landing page promises free use for shoppers in the Berlin pilot, which keeps adoption friction low while the revenue options (store subscription, promoted products, premium, chain partnership) are validated with retailers.
ProductWhy the name PeekShop?It expresses the promise directly: a quick peek at the shelf before you go, subject to trademark clearance.

Other Considerations

Dependencies — scenarios worth tracking during design/engineering planning

Dependency scenarioDescriptionWhy it matters / risk if unmet
1. Supply must exist before demandStore onboarding (PS-21) and published stock (PS-22, PS-23) must exist before search results (PS-8) are useful.A thin or empty result set ruins first impressions. Launch in a few districts and categories with dense coverage.
2. Location and account shape every resultSign-up and log-in (PS-1, PS-2) and location permission (PS-3) drive nearby results (PS-8, PS-18).Without permission, the manual area picker must give an equivalent path, not a dead end.
3. Freshness drives trust“Updated X min ago” (PS-10) needs a timestamp from every update path (PS-22, PS-23, PS-24) and the staleness job (PS-25).A record without a timestamp cannot be trusted; treat it as outdated rather than show it as fresh.
4. Filters depend on data completenessOpen now (PS-9) needs accurate hours (PS-21), Quiet needs crowd data (PS-26) and Under 1 km needs location (PS-3).When the data is missing, hide or disable the filter instead of guessing.
5. Similar products need taxonomyAlternatives (PS-11) need the category taxonomy (PS-6.1), similarity rules (PS-11.1) and in-stock data (PS-8).Poor matches (a different brand family or size) erode trust in suggestions.
6. Reservations need live stock and a store that actsReserve (PS-15) needs live stock (PS-8), an open store (PS-12) and a store-side list (PS-17); cancel (PS-16) and expiry release stock.Holds that stores do not see create no-shows and angry shoppers.
7. Aisle and shelf need retailer setupThe floor plan (PS-13) needs aisle and shelf fields and floor-plan setup in the portal (PS-13.1, PS-13.3).Hide the floor plan when no data exists so nothing breaks.
8. Directions depend on the map providerWalking directions (PS-19) build on the map (PS-18) and a hand-off to native maps.Provider choice and usage cost must be settled in Sprint 0.
9. The feedback loop needs trip signals“Was it on the shelf?” (PS-29) is asked after directions (PS-19) or a reservation (PS-15); “No” notifies the store and flags the record.Without it there is no measured stock accuracy, the core trust metric.
10. Analytics must respect consentFunnel events (PS-27) feed activation and the North Star metric but fire only after consent.Sending personal data without consent breaches GDPR and the privacy principle.
11. Localisation is cross-cuttingEnglish and German (NFR-07) affect permission copy (PS-3.1), errors, empty states and store-facing messages.Every new feature must ship with both languages or it silently breaks the promise.
12. External services are a shared riskMaps (PS-18), notifications (PS-15.3, PS-17.3, PS-25.1), search (PS-5), POS (PS-24) and monitoring (PS-28).Each must degrade gracefully: if a notification fails, the state stays visible in the app or portal.

Privacy & GDPR

Location, account and reservation data are personal data. Location is requested with a clear reason and a manual alternative (PS-3), analytics events carry no personal data without consent (PS-27), and the product supports data export and deletion, data minimisation and EU data hosting (NFR-05). A GDPR (DSGVO) review is a launch gate. Any new feature that processes personal data should be checked against this model before it enters the backlog.

Reliability & error handling

On failure (upload, update, reserve, directions), data must stay consistent and the user must be able to retry. A CSV upload publishes valid rows and lists invalid rows with reasons (PS-22). Cancelled and expired reservations release stock (PS-15, PS-16). When freshness is unknown, we say so. Treat this as a house style for error handling rather than deciding it per feature.

Empty states

Several features explicitly need an informative empty state instead of a blank screen: no search match with example searches (PS-5), no store matching the filters with a Clear filters button (PS-9), an empty Saved tab that explains how to save (PS-20), and a hidden floor plan or crowd indicator when there is no data (PS-13, PS-26). This belongs in the shared design system.

Accessibility & localisation

The app targets WCAG 2.1 AA: 4.5:1 text contrast, 44 px touch targets, screen-reader labels and no meaning carried by colour alone (stock status always has a text label). English and German ship together, text is externalised for more languages, and all new copy, including notifications and error messages, needs both variants before a feature can ship.
